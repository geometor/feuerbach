1. Did Feuerbach Already Construct the Euler Midpoints in 1822?
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

A widespread claim in mathematical history asserts that :person:`Feuerbach <feuerbach>` noticed only *six* points on the nine-point circle (the three altitude feet and three side midpoints) and that the three Euler midpoints were only added later by Olry Terquem (1842).

**Inspection of page 62 (`9. Satz`) refutes that claim!**

Feuerbach's synthetic construction explicitly draws line :math:`DL` from side midpoint :math:`D = \operatorname{mid}(AB)` through :math:`L = \operatorname{mid}(OK)` to meet altitude :math:`CP` at :math:`J`. By congruent triangles :math:`\triangle OLJ \cong \triangle KLD` :just:`[I. 26] <I.26>`:

* :math:`OJ = KD = \frac{1}{2}CO` — **so** :math:`J` **is literally the Euler midpoint** :g:`E_3 <0038.g1:E3>` bisecting :math:`CO`!
* :math:`JL = DL = \frac{1}{2}CK = \frac{1}{2}R` — **so** :math:`J` lies at distance :math:`\frac{1}{2}R` from :math:`L`, diametrically opposite :math:`D`!

2. Reflection Unfolding in 8. Satz (Schwarz Anticipation)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

In `8. Satz` (pp. 61–62), dropping perpendiculars :math:`PG, PH` from an altitude foot :math:`P` onto the other two sides and prolonging them to :math:`J, K` unfolds the perimeter of :math:`\triangle MNP` into a single straight line :math:`JK = 2\,GH`. This reflection construction directly anticipated H. A. Schwarz's famous 1890 reflection solution of :person:`Fagnano <fagnano>`'s problem!
