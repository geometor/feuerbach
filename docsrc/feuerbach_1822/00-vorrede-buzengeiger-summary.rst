===============================================================
Summary — Unit 0: Karl Buzengeiger's *Vorrede* (pp. III–XVI)
===============================================================

.. rubric:: Philosophical & Methodological Framing of Feuerbach's 1822 Monograph

1. **Dual Aspect of Mathematics (pp. III–IV)**: :person:`Karl Buzengeiger <buzengeiger>` opens with a classical distinction between mathematics as deductive *science* (*Wissenschaft* — exemplified above all by :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s :cite:`*Elements* <euclid_elements>`) and mathematics as heuristic *art* (*Kunst* — exemplified by Greek geometric *analysis* in :cite:`Euclid's *Data* <euclid_data>`, :person:`Apollonius <apollonius>` as restored by Robert Simson, and the arithmetical *Arithmetica* of Diophantus).
2. **Critique of Combinatorial Notation & Factorials (p. XV)**: Reflecting on early nineteenth-century German mathematics, Buzengeiger praises analytical factorials (*Fakultäten*, studied by Kramp and Bessel) for evaluating intractable integrals while sharply criticizing the Hindenburg combinatorial school's “repulsive and unserviceable wilderness of notation” (*Zeichenwust*). By contrast, :person:`Feuerbach <feuerbach>`'s monograph employs clean, symmetric algebraic-trigonometric notation in the spirit of :person:`Euler <euler>` and :person:`L'Huilier <lhuilier>`.
3. **The Inexhaustible Plane Triangle (p. XVI)**: Answering the fear that elementary geometry had been exhausted since antiquity, Buzengeiger points directly to his twenty-one-year-old pupil's discovery: even the rectilinear triangle—the simplest figure in plane geometry—still harbors unsuspected structural theorems of the first rank when investigated by a “thoroughly systematic mind” (*einen sehr systematischen Kopf*).
