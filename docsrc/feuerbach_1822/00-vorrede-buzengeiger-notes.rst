======================================================================
Critical Notes — Unit 0: Karl Buzengeiger's *Vorrede* (pp. III–XVI)
======================================================================

.. rubric:: Historical Context at Freiburg & Erlangen (1820–1822)

1. **Feuerbach's Academic Formation**: :person:`Karl Wilhelm Feuerbach <feuerbach>` (born 30 May 1800 in Jena, son of the jurist Paul Johann Anselm Ritter von Feuerbach and brother of the philosopher Ludwig Feuerbach) studied first at the University of Erlangen and transferred in 1820 to the University of Freiburg im Breisgau, where he studied under :person:`Karl Heribert Ignaz Buzengeiger <buzengeiger>` (1771–1835). Returning to Erlangen to take his doctorate of philosophy at age twenty-two, Feuerbach published *Eigenschaften einiger merkwürdigen Punkte des geradlinigen Dreiecks* with Riegel und Wießner in Nuremberg in the spring of 1822.
2. **Synthesis vs. Analysis inside the Monograph**: Buzengeiger's preface directly anticipates the two-part architecture of Feuerbach's treatise:

   * **Analytisch-trigonometrische Abhandlung (§§ 1–87, pp. 1–55)**: Systematic symmetric algebra in :math:`(a, b, c, \Delta, R, r, r', r'', r''', \rho)`, where replacing :math:`a \mapsto -a` (with :math:`\alpha \mapsto -\alpha` or :math:`\pi - \alpha`) carries incircle relations into excircle relations.
   * **Synthetischer Anhang (1.–9. Satz, pp. 56–62)**: Pure Euclidean synthesis demonstrating the orthic area ratio, altitude chord product, and nine-point radius :math:`\tfrac{1}{2}R` directly from Books I, III, and VI of :cite:`Euclid's *Elements* <euclid_elements>` (mirroring :person:`Pappus <pappus>`'s *Synagoge* Book VII pairing of *Analysis* and *Synthesis*).

3. **Simson's Restorations**: Buzengeiger's reference on p. III to Robert Simson (1687–1768) alludes to Simson's celebrated Latin reconstructions of Apollonius's *Loci Plani* (1749) and Euclid's *Porisms* and *Data* (1762), which served alongside Euler's *Novi Commentarii* memoirs as primary models for early nineteenth-century German triangle geometry.
