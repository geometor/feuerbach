First Section: On the Centers of the Four Tritangent Circles (§§ 1–18)¶

Karl Wilhelm Feuerbach — Properties of Several Remarkable Points of the Rectilinear Triangle (1822, pp. 1–14, Fig. 1)

Section I establishes the metric foundations of the four tritangent circles (one incircle, three excircles). Feuerbach discovers that triangle ABC is the orthic (pedal) triangle of the excentral triangle S’S’’S’’’, with incenter S as its orthocenter (§ 1). He proves L’Huilier’s theorem rr’r’’r’’’ = Delta^2 (§ 2), establishes elementary symmetric polynomial identities among the exradii (§§ 3–7), proves that contact triangle areas scale linearly with the tritangent radii (§§ 8–11), and deduces the metric laws governing the orthocentric quadrangle (S, S’, S’’, S’’’) with opposite distance-square sums equal to (4R)^2 = 16R^2 (§§ 12–18).

Sections¶

§ 1: The Four Tritangent Circles and the Excentral Orthocentric System

As is well known, for any triangle \(ABC\) whatever, four distinct circles touching its three sides [tritangent circles] are possible, of which only one lies within, whereas the remaining three lie outside the triangle (Fig. 1). The first internally touching or inscribed circle touches all three sides of the triangle itself and lies simultaneously in the planes [interior sectors] of all three of its angles. Each of the three latter externally touching circles, on the contrary, touches only one of the three sides itself together with the prolongations of the other two, and lies in the sector of that angle which stands opposite the side touched by itself.
